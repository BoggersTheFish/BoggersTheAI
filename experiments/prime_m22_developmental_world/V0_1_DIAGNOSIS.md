# PRIME M22 Developmental Laboratory v0.1 Diagnosis

Status:

    DEVELOPMENT FAILURE PRESERVED

The first M22 developmental experiment compared:

    COLD PRIME

against:

    PERSISTENT PRIME

across 25 sequential developmental chapters.

Results:

    cold recovery:
        25 / 25

    persistent recovery:
        20 / 25

    comparable recoveries:
        18

    mean transfer gain:
        +11 events

    positive / tied / negative:
        17 / 1 / 0

    mean AULC gain:
        -37837 ppm

Persistent cognition accumulated:

    12 semantic-memory classes
    25 episodic memories
    valid episodic hash chain
    3 schema proposals

Schemas:

    AND offsets (0,3), support 4
    XOR offsets (0,3), support 3
    OR  offsets (0,3), support 3

The failure was highly structured.

Persistent PRIME failed to recover:

    XOR-1-2-3
    XOR-2-3-4
    XOR-3-4-5
    AND-1-2-4
    OR-2-3-4

These are higher-order constructions.

The persistent engine reduced the candidate field from the cold universal
256-candidate field to 64 candidates whenever prior memory existed.

As persistent memory grew, prior proposals occupied increasing fractions of
this 64-candidate field.

The result was representational search collapse:

    prior knowledge improved discovery latency for hypotheses that remained
    available;

but:

    prior knowledge removed novel higher-order hypotheses from consideration.

This is negative transfer caused by overconfident proposal-space compression.

The result falsifies the naive rule:

    accumulated knowledge
        ->
    globally shrink future search space.

It motivates a stronger architecture:

    accumulated knowledge
        ->
    redistribute search/evidence budget

while maintaining universal hypothesis coverage.

The next M22 architecture therefore replaces hard candidate truncation with a
weighted hypothesis ecology.

Memory may increase evidence budget for likely hypotheses.

It may not delete the universal safety field.

No M22 held-out evaluation has been performed.
